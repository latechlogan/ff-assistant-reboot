# Decisions & learnings

Append-only log. One dated line per entry: what was decided or learned, and why it
matters. Claude appends here when something surprising happens or a choice forecloses
alternatives; /wrap prompts for entries at session end. Read this before debugging
anything strange.

Format: `- YYYY-MM-DD — [decision|learning] entry`

- 2026-09-17 — [decision] ff-assistant is rebuilt from scratch as ff-assistant-reboot rather than refactored in place. The original was not short on tests (653 passing) but on organization: a spec that had become an amendment log, no stated module ownership, draft and in-season concerns tangled, 14 CLI stages coupled through a JSON cache with a silent 24h TTL, and no lint/format/CI. Logan's own reasons: he never guided or critiqued the agent-written tests, so a green run carried no trust, and the stack kept creeping to accommodate work that had not been discussed.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The architecture is the filter for new features. A proposed feature either fits the documented design and stack — in which case it is built — or it does not, in which case the conversation is about changing the design, with Logan in it. Nothing morphs silently.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] v1 is the in-season waiver board only. The draft board and live draft assistant are 2027 work, planned for in the architecture but not built this season.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] v1 ships two separate columns, not one recommendation: value (rest-of-season worth, survival-weighted, in FAAB dollars and percent of budget) and bid range (what the winning bid is likely to be, measured from real guillotine rooms). Logan's framing: the gap between them is the decision, like a P/E ratio.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The bid range is a range because measured winning bids run ~1.4× the runner-up (median 0.71× second bid across 23 rooms). A single number would be false precision about a price the winner overpaid.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The standard league stays in v1 despite having no currency to price. Its week-2 board earned it: one positive-VORP player meant one decision instead of an evening of digging. Triage is the feature.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] League settings come from each league's Sleeper snapshot from day one; supporting both leagues costs almost nothing. What costs is supporting a new *format* — guillotine vs. standard — so only formats Logan actually plays are supported. No other users, no other leagues.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Claim-now-or-wait, roster-tailored value, and rival-budget-aware bid suggestions are wanted but deferred past v1; predicting individual managers' bids and validating edge against results are rejected outright (too little data, n=1 season).
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Projections stay free and single-source in-season (Sleeper's weekly stat lines, which are RotoWire's). Paid projections were considered and declined; the larger lever on bid quality is the market model, not the forecast. Logan would sooner spend on storage that unlocks features than on a projection feed.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Runs on Logan's Mac, on demand. No hosting, no scheduled job: Sleeper retains bid history, so a skipped week loses nothing, and the decision is human anyway.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The old repo is deleted, not archived (Logan's call; the trade — losing the git history's why-trail — was named). Before deletion, its irreplaceable data was copied into `data/archive/` with a README: per-source draft-day projections that existed only in a gitignored cache, raw Sleeper league/draft/auction snapshots, the frozen week-2 waiver boards, week 1–2 weekly projections and transactions, and the 2025 guillotine bid research.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Nothing in `data/archive/` is an input until it is re-derived here. The 2025 guillotine bid research was produced by the old repo's agent-written scripts, unreviewed; the bid model is re-measured in this repo with our own crawl and our own tests, and the archived tables become the sanity check — agreement confirms them, disagreement is investigated. Integrity of the archived files (parse, counts, reconciliation, drift against live Sleeper) is verified separately, on arrival.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Value is priced in FAAB dollars only; percent-of-full-budget is the same number scaled and was dropped. The second lens is percent of Logan's *remaining* FAAB, applied to the bid range. Budget-aware display (a different denominator) is v1; roster-aware valuation (different math) stays deferred.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] A display column added on a hunch carries a stated trial period — a few real Tuesdays — after which it is kept or cut, and the cut is logged. The rule exists because the 2026 tool accumulated features like the position-inflation tracker that sounded useful, were built, went unused, and were only cut much later.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The tool accepts any Sleeper league ID and prices it from its own settings (any team count, budget, or roster shape), or refuses it at the boundary with a named reason. Unsupported formats — superflex, IDP, DST, best-ball, dynasty, non-matching guillotine cadence — fail loudly rather than being approximated. Generalizing the *settings* is nearly free; generalizing the *format* is not, so it is refused instead of guessed.

Logan's reason for public is professional evidence — reps with the tools, shown through the planning docs, tests and decision trail rather than through the pricing math. Data stays out because it contains real managers' names and other people's leagues, and because flipping to public later would require rewriting git history.
- 2026-09-17 — [learning] Archive verified on arrival (see `data/archive/README.md`): files intact, 2026 draft picks match the live Sleeper API exactly, league settings show no drift. Two findings change the plan. (2) The archived chopped draft board is an 18-team board with a $3,600 pool against a room that had 16 teams and $3,200; it is the board that was really used on draft night, and any parity check against it has to account for that.
- 2026-09-17 — [learning] The bid research's method was read and holds up on the obvious failure modes (per-league budget normalization, no double counting, failed claims never counted as wins). Its weaknesses are the ones to design around when we re-measure: a snowball sample rooted in Logan's own leaguemates, losing bids found by string-matching Sleeper's rejection copy, top-bid statistics conditioned on weeks where somebody won a claim, and a hardcoded season-start date for week bucketing.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Stack: TypeScript on Node 24 LTS with Node's own type stripping (no build step, no `tsx`), `tsc --noEmit` plus `erasableSyntaxOnly` for checking, Zod at every boundary, pnpm 10, Vitest, Prettier + ESLint, dependency-cruiser, gitleaks, stdlib for CLI flags and HTTP. Chosen for familiarity and for consistency with ff-weekly, the only other active repo — one mental model across both.

- 2026-09-17 — [decision] No database in v1; plain JSON files. `node:sqlite` is the named upgrade path because it costs no dependency. Storage is deliberately not committed to now, since a Python measurement cycle would point at DuckDB instead.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Python (pandas/DuckDB) for the bid measurement is deferred to the cycle after v1, not rejected. Logan wants the skill and it is the better tool for the analysis; the cost is a second toolchain against a week-3 deadline, and v1's measurement is small enough for TypeScript. The batch-versus-serving seam (offline measurement emits a reviewed JSON artifact; online pricing consumes it) means adopting it later touches one side only.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Rejected: Biome (weaker type-aware linting, new config, speed irrelevant here — declined once already in the ff-weekly kickoff), Bun and Deno (this tool waits on the network, not the CPU, and Node 24 already runs TypeScript natively; the runtime is the most expensive thing to change later), `tsx` (now unnecessary).
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] League data lives in a private sibling repo (`ff-assistant-data`) reached through one configured data root, not in the public code repo and not as a git submodule — a submodule would embed a private pointer in a public repo and break clones for anyone without access.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Data model (see `docs/data-model.md`): no database, all state in files. Store only what arrived from outside, plus three deliberate artifacts — frozen boards, the measured bid curve, and the raw payloads themselves. Everything else (league config, scored points, replacement level, VORP, values, the available pool) is recomputed every run, and no derived artifact is ever read back as a pricing input.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Caches have no TTL. A fetch writes a new as-of file, nothing is overwritten, an existing file is used as-is, and fresh data requires an explicit `--refresh`. The 2026 tool's 24h expiry meant a rerun a day later silently refetched and the numbers moved, so measuring a change required freezing the clock; immutable as-of snapshots remove the clock from the pipeline entirely.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Every written artifact carries `schemaVersion` and a reader that meets an unknown version refuses loudly. No migration tool: past weeks are immutable, so a shape change bumps the version and applies forward only. League-payload validation is also where an unsupported format is rejected with a named reason.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] Tests run against trimmed real Sleeper payloads, regenerated by `pnpm fixtures` from the private data repo and scrubbed: fake owner IDs, display names and league IDs; real NFL player names kept, since name-matching tests are worthless without them. A privacy scan in the check command fails on any real handle, owner ID or league ID in a tracked file — gitleaks catches credentials, not a leaguemate's handle.
- 2026-09-17 — [decision] The archived 2026 week-2 board is a one-time parity check for the value column, then retired; the permanent golden test pins our own first board instead. - 2026-09-17 — [decision] League IDs live in `leagues.json` in the private data repo, not in the public code repo. Retention: every weekly payload is kept (~40 MB a season) so any week stays reproducible; player maps are kept per pull date and pruned once no frozen board references them.

- 2026-09-18 — [decision] Architecture is ports and adapters (see `docs/architecture.md`): a pure core (scoring, survival, valuation, bid pricing, board assembly) with no `fetch`, no `node:fs`, no clock, no env, and adapters at every edge (Sleeper, crawl, store, CLI, logger). Chosen because it makes pricing testable without a network, makes a later UI an adapter rather than a rewrite, and makes determinism structural — a core with no clock cannot drift between runs. Enforced by a dependency-cruiser rule in the check command, not by a paragraph.
- 2026-09-18 — [decision] Day-one interface is a CLI, one league per invocation, with `--league` required. Each run's output, exit code, and frozen artifact then describe exactly one league. Flags: `--week`, `--refresh`, `--all`, `--debug`.

- 2026-09-18 — [learning] The core-purity rules were proven by writing a deliberate violation before trusting them: depcruise caught the `node:fs` and adapter imports, ESLint caught `fetch`, `Date` and `Math.random` in `src/core`. A guard nobody has watched fail is a guard of unknown value.
- 2026-09-18 — [decision] `pnpm check` runs prettier → eslint (type-aware) → tsc → depcruise → vitest → privacy-scan → gitleaks, and passes on an empty `src/`. The privacy scan flags any bare 15-20 digit Sleeper ID in a tracked file and, when the private data repo is present, any term in its `privacy-denylist.json` — the denylist itself can never live in the public repo, since that would be the leak.
- 2026-09-18 — [learning] Node's type stripping rejects constructor parameter properties (`constructor(private readonly x: T)`) — the first real cost of running `.ts` without a build step. `tsc --noEmit` with `erasableSyntaxOnly` reports it as TS1294, so the check command catches it before a run does. Fields are declared and assigned explicitly instead; no other pattern in the codebase was affected.
- 2026-09-18 — [learning] Modern Sleeper PLAYER ids are 18-19 digit numbers (a rookie's is 1113708741519241216), the same shape as league, draft and owner ids. No pattern can tell a public player id from a private owner id, so the first privacy scan — which flagged long digit runs — was both noisy and unsound.
- 2026-09-18 — [decision] Privacy scanning is exact, not shape-based: `../ff-assistant-data/privacy-denylist.json` holds the real identifiers, `pnpm privacy-hashes` publishes their SHA-256 to `privacy-hashes.json` here, and the scan hashes every candidate it finds. Hashes leak nothing about a 19-digit id nobody can enumerate, and CI gets the same check the laptop has. When the private repo is present the plaintext terms are checked too, which also catches handles. Proven by planting a real league id in a tracked file and watching the check fail.

- 2026-09-18 — [decision] Prettier skips `test/fixtures/` as well as markdown: fixtures are machine-written, and reformatting them would produce diff noise on every regeneration.
- 2026-09-18 — [learning] The Stop hook reported "No stderr output" while `pnpm check` was actually failing: pnpm prints command failures on stdout, and hook feedback only surfaces stderr. The hook was blocking the turn and the failure was invisible. Fixed by redirecting stdout to stderr in the hook (`pnpm check 1>&2 || exit 2`). - 2026-09-18 — [decision] ESLint treats a leading underscore as "deliberately unused" for arguments, variables and caught errors. Stub signatures carry their parameters for the contract's sake and cannot use them yet, and the alternative — disabling the rule — would hide real dead parameters later.
- 2026-09-18 — [decision] `survivalWeights`' `weeksRemaining` means the rest of the SEASON, not an arbitrary window, and the capacity guard (a league cannot need more chops than its weeks can deliver) stays inside the pure function.
